Use case: ui-mockup.
Create ONE high-fidelity visual design review board for a student assessment management web app. This is a new exploratory concept, not an implementation. Landscape 3:2 composition, high resolution, three equally sized large mobile app screen panels side by side, big readable interface text. No real phone hardware, no photographic desk, no perspective distortion. Neutral light warm background, slim vertical dividers, graceful generous margins. This board must communicate one coherent three-state interaction with the same assessment, not three alternative concepts.

Design direction: a warm, tactile card-building toy translated into a mature, very approachable student planner. Ivory #FAF8F1, dark teal #215D52, pale lavender assessment card #E7DDF2, pale sage and apricot secondary accents. Serif page headings, clean readable sans-serif controls. Rounded paper cards, subtle layered shadows, physically pressable teal buttons. Calm and welcoming, no corporate dashboard density. Game feel through direct manipulation and card placement, NOT XP, coins, scores, badges, streaks, mascots, levels, punishment, or busy decoration. Realistic implementable UI. Every action possible with taps and keyboard; don't require drag.

Outside the app panels at the top: English title "One card at a time" with small subtitle "A gentler first step". Above the three panels number labels: "01  Name it", "02  Give it a date", "03  See what is next". Thin simple arrows in the gaps connect the stages. All visible text English, correctly spelled. No Japanese.

PANEL 1 — initial empty signed-in semester, very little UI. Header small "Assessments." logo word and a gear icon. H1 "My semester". One explanatory sentence "What do you need to hand in?" Main central object a LARGE blank pale ivory/lavender paper card, containing a single spacious input with placeholder "e.g. SQL exercises" and an attached teal Add button with plus icon. Label "Assessment name". Under the input small plain helper "Unit and date can come later." No unit choice, no Save, no form wizard, no draft label, no login form. Below the card one quiet secondary link "See an example". Bottom plain text navigation "My semester" (active) and "Submitted". Filling the name and pressing Add or Enter is the one main action.

PANEL 2 — immediately after adding. Same header and heading. Show the same card now titled "SQL exercises", pale lavender, modest tactile raised edge, with two rounded outlined optional chips "+ Unit" and "+ Date". Tiny transient "Added" acknowledgement. Indicate a fingertip cursor tapping "+ Date", using a small drawn cursor, not a human hand. Below, a compact date picker bottom sheet is visibly open, title "When is it due?", with text input "2026-10-16", a small neat October 2026 calendar grid with 16 selected, full date readback "Friday 16 October 2026", teal "Use this date" button and subtle "Leave date unset" link. The date sheet is a UI overlay within this panel and must not obscure the SQL exercises title. Keep legibility more important than showing every calendar row; can show week of 12,13,14,15,16,17,18 clearly instead of full dense month. Assessment unit remains optional; no invented extra assessment.

PANEL 3 — saved visible outcome. Same header "My semester". Prominent small plain sentence "Next up — SQL exercises · Due today". A tiny date header "FRIDAY 16 OCTOBER". The SAME lavender card sits neatly aligned in the due-today area, with "SQL exercises", optional unit chip "+ Unit", high-contrast teal "Due today" pill, small date "16 Oct 2026". A clear tactile action button "Mark submitted". A quiet row "+ Add another assessment" beneath. No extra assessments or fake metrics. Bottom tabs "My semester" active and "Submitted". No save button, no technical sync/backup/revision language. Preserve an accessible gear settings entry. No invented achievements or confetti cloud. Small paper placement accent lines are okay.

At bottom of entire board outside app UI, three short captions aligned with screens: "Type a name. Press Enter." / "Tap a date when you know it." / "Your next step stays visible."
Do not include cloud setup, billing, authentication changes, generated code, explanatory marketing paragraphs, oversized dashboard charts or decorative empty sidebars. The aim is that an inexperienced person understands the first action at a glance and feels the reward of their assessment becoming a tangible card.
